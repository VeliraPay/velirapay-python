# Changelog

All notable changes to this library are documented here. It follows [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org).

## Unreleased

- Refund requests, through eight new methods on `charges`: `refund_requests()` (a `Page` of the charge's refund requests, newest first), `retrieve_refund_request()`, `start_refund()`, `set_refund_address()`, `mark_refund_sent()`, `complete_refund()`, `resend_refund_link()` and `cancel_refund()`. Each takes the charge's id, the ones that act on a refund request its id too, and the writes an `idempotency_key`. `start_refund()` sets an amount of the charge's coin aside, so nothing can refund it twice, and, unless it is given the `address`, asks the customer for one: the link is emailed to the charge's customer email when it has one, and is the request's `address_url` either way, working for 14 days. You send the refund from your own wallet, VeliraPay follows the transfer to the address, or the hash you give `mark_refund_sent()`, and records the refund once it has its confirmations, with `charge.refunded` and the customer's email as before. `complete_refund()` records it on your word, with or without a `txid`, for a transfer the chain cannot be seen to carry, such as one in Monero. Their parameters are typed as `RefundRequestCreateParams`, `RefundAddressParams`, `RefundSentParams` and `RefundCompleteParams`, and the amount is sent as a decimal string, as elsewhere. They need `charges:refund`, reads included, and a call the refund request no longer takes raises a `ConflictError`.
- `RefundRequest`, with `id`, `charge`, `status`, `amount`, `reason`, `address`, `address_given_by` (`"customer"` or `"merchant"`), `address_url`, `link_expires_at`, `emailed_at`, `txid`, `detected_amount`, `confirmations`, `refund` (the `Refund` it was recorded as, or `None`), `created_at`, `completed_at` and `canceled_at`, and `is_open()` and `is_refunded()`. `RefundRequestStatus` gives its statuses: `awaiting_address`, `awaiting_transfer`, `confirming`, `refunded`, `canceled` and `expired`.
- `Refund`, in `charge.refunds` and a refund request's `refund`, gains `number`, its credit note's number such as `"CN-0001"`, `fiat_amount`, what it is worth in the charge's currency at the charge's locked rate, `credit_note_url`, the printable credit note, and `confirmed_on_chain_at`, a `datetime` when the chain showed it landing and `None` when it was recorded on your word. All four are `None` in a payload that leaves them out.
- Tax and discounts on invoices. `invoices.create()` takes a `tax_rate` on each item, in percent from 0 to 100, and, with items, a `discount_percent` or a `discount_amount`, never both, taken off the items before tax, typed in `InvoiceItemParams` and `InvoiceCreateParams`; each is sent as a decimal string. The API ignored all three before 2026-11-26, so an invoice created with them was for the items' plain sum: it now honours them, and the invoice's `amount`, what the customer is charged, is the items less the discount plus the tax. It also checks them now, so a call it used to take raises a `ValidationError` and creates nothing when it sends a discount with `amount` rather than items, both discounts together, or a tax rate or discount out of range or with more than two decimals.
- `Invoice` gains `subtotal`, `discount`, `discount_percent` and `tax`, and `InvoiceItem` gains `tax_rate`, as strings, in the API's answers and in webhooks alike. They are `None` for an invoice without a breakdown and in a payload that leaves them out. `InvoiceItem.total` stays the quantity times the unit amount, before tax.
- Added the activity actions `refund.requested`, `refund.canceled`, `accounting.connected` and `accounting.disconnected`, the two accounting ones among the sensitive ones.

## 0.2.8 - 2026-11-24

- Added the activity actions `auth.browser_signed_out`, `auth.email_change_undone`, `claude.linked` and `claude.unlinked`, all four among the sensitive ones.
- `charge.accepted_at`, and `paid_at` and `accepted_at` on each of `invoice.charges`, in the API and in webhooks: when the merchant accepted a short or late payment as paid, which is how the charge became paid, and null when the payment itself covered it. Check it before fulfilling an order, since a payment may be accepted only to refund it.
- `InvoiceCharge` gains `paid_at` and `accepted_at`, both `None` unless given.
- The unused part of a payout's network reserve is described as kept as part of the payout's network cost, which is what the settlement terms now say for every payment.

## 0.2.7 - 2026-11-20

- Added `Asset.USDT_SOLANA` and `Asset.USDC_SOLANA` and the seven activity actions for team creation and deletion, account deletion, recovery codes and data exports.
- Clarified how the payment's settlement terms apply to unused network reserves.

## 0.2.6 - 2026-11-17

- Typed checkout questions. `payment_links.create()` and `update()` take, for each of up to ten `custom_fields`, a `type` (a member of the new `QuestionType` or its value: `text`, `textarea`, `number`, `email`, `phone`, `url`, `date`, `select`, `checkbox` or `country`), a `placeholder`, a `help` line, a select's `options`, `min` and `max` as strings, and for text a `pattern` with its `pattern_hint`. Only `label` is needed now (`CheckoutQuestionParams` is `total=False` with `label` required): `{"label": …, "required": …}` alone is still a short text question, as before.
- `CheckoutQuestion`, what `link.custom_fields` holds, gains `type`, `placeholder`, `help`, `options`, `min`, `max`, `pattern` and `pattern_hint`: `type` is `"text"` for a link saved before types, and the settings a question does not use are `None`. `CustomFieldAnswer`, what `charge.custom_fields` holds in the API and in webhooks, gains `type`, `"text"` for details given when creating the charge or kept before types; `value` stays a string. Both new sets of fields come after the old ones with defaults, so `CheckoutQuestion(label=…, required=…)` and `CustomFieldAnswer(label=…, value=…)` still work.
- Lightning is removed: the `BTC_LIGHTNING` asset, the `lightning` payout address network (`AddressNetwork.LIGHTNING`) and `Charge.payment_request` are gone, because VeliraPay no longer offers Bitcoin over Lightning. The API never returned them for anyone, since no account ever had Lightning switched on.

## 0.2.5 - 2026-11-15

- An invoice must name its customer: `invoices.create()` takes `customer_name`, `customer_email` or both, and a name alone, such as the buyer's username on your site, is enough. The API refuses an invoice with neither, so `create()` raises a `ValidationError` on `customer_name` ("Name the customer, give their email, or both.") and creates nothing. This replaces 0.2.4's invoice that names nobody and can be paid by whoever has its link. `customer_email` is still needed with `send_email`, and an invoice without one still cannot be sent or reminded.
- Nothing changes in the library's types or at runtime: both stay optional in `InvoiceCreateParams`, since either may be left out, and `invoice.customer_name` and `invoice.customer_email` stay `str | None`, `customer_name` being `None` for an invoice given only an email and `customer_email` for one given only a name. The README and the docstrings now say an invoice names its customer, and no longer that one can name nobody.

## 0.2.4 - 2026-11-15

- `invoices.create()` no longer requires `customer_name` and `customer_email`: either can be left out or set to `None`, and an invoice that names nobody can be paid by whoever has its link. `customer_email` is still needed with `send_email`: without it `create()` raises a `ValidationError` on `customer_email` and creates nothing, and `invoices.send()` raises the same for an invoice that has none. Calls that give both work as before.
- `invoices.create()` takes `expires_at`, when the invoice stops taking new payments, as payment links and coupons do: a `datetime`, sent in UTC, a `date`, which ends at 23:59:59 UTC that day, or an ISO 8601 string, read in the offset it names. It must be in the future. A payment the customer already started can still be finished in its coin, `invoices.send()` raises a `ConflictError` once the invoice has expired, and an invoice still unpaid once it has closed, with no payment under way, is voided on its own: `status` `"void"`, its `voided_at` and an `invoice.voided` webhook. `invoice.expires_at` reads it back as a `datetime`, and is `None` for an invoice that stays open until it is paid or voided.
- `invoices.create()` takes `success_message`, up to 1,000 characters: what the customer is told once they have paid, on the invoice's page and in their receipt. Left out, the account's own message applies. `invoice.success_message` reads it back.
- `invoice.customer_name` and `invoice.customer_email` are typed `str | None`, where they were `str`, and are `None` for an invoice that names nobody or has no email address, where a missing value read as `""`. A type checker asks code that uses them as strings to handle `None`; nothing changes at runtime for an invoice that has both.
- The invoices in `invoice.*` webhooks carry `expires_at`, `overdue`, `document_url`, `success_url`, `cancel_url` and `success_message`, with the same values as the API's. A delivery queued before the API added them, such as a resend of an older one, leaves them out, and they read as `None`.

## 0.2.3 - 2026-11-14

- `charge.switched_from`, an `OriginalQuote` with `asset`, `asset_amount`, `exchange_rate` and `switched_at` (a `datetime`), in the API's answers, the charges events carry and webhooks alike. TRX and USDT on Tron share deposit addresses, and when a charge in one of them closes, expired or canceled, with nothing of its own coin while the other arrived at its address, VeliraPay quotes it again in the coin that arrived: `asset`, `asset_amount`, `exchange_rate` and `network_surcharge` then describe that coin, `fiat_amount` stays the same, and `switched_from` keeps the first quote. The transfer waits as a late payment (`charge.late_payment`) for the merchant to accept, and `charge.paid` and what follows it are in the new coin. A charge switches at most once, so `charge.asset` can change once: read the coin from each payload rather than the one the charge was created with. `switched_from` is `None` for every other charge, and in a payload that leaves it out. Its amounts carry all 18 decimal places in webhooks, like the charge's own.
- `charge.processing_terms`, a `ProcessingTerms` with `percent`, the processing fee fixed when the charge was quoted, in percent of what is paid and leaving out any network surcharge, a string such as `"1.75"`, and `max_forwarding_percent`, the most managed forwarding may cost, in percent of the merchant's share after the fee, an `int` such as `10`. It is `None` for older charges, VeliraPay's own billing and a payload that leaves it out.
- `payout.merchant_network_reserve`, a `NetworkReserve`, in the API's answers and in the `payout.sent` and `payout.confirmed` webhooks, with the same amounts in both. Under the processing terms charges carry, the merchant's share pays the network costs of forwarding it, and when a payout's exact cost is not known as it goes out, such as one in ETH, in TRX or over Lightning, an estimate is set aside from that share first, in the payout's coin. `reserved` is what was set aside, `payout_cost` what forwarding cost, from its receipt, `sweep_budget` and `sweep_cost` what was set aside for moving VeliraPay's fee to its cold wallet and what that cost, `sweep_available` what is left of that budget, `held` what is still held for the merchant, counted conservatively, and `residual` what went unused once every cost is known, which stays owed to the merchant and is recorded rather than sent back. All are strings; `payout_cost`, `sweep_cost` and `residual` are `None` until they are known. `merchant_network_reserve` is `None` for older charges, payouts not yet sent, payouts whose cost is known up front, such as in the Bitcoin family or in USDT and USDC, and a payload that leaves it out.
- All three are read only, and `OriginalQuote`, `ProcessingTerms` and `NetworkReserve` are importable from `velirapay`.

## 0.2.2 - 2026-11-12

- `charge.customer.ip_country`, `ip_asn` and `ip_network`: what VeliraPay works out from the customer's IP address, in the API's answers and in webhooks alike. `ip_country` is the country the hosted checkout placed the address in, as an ISO 3166 two-letter code or `T1` for Tor, and `None` for a charge created through the API; `ip_asn` is the number of the network (autonomous system) the address belongs to, an `int`, and `ip_network` who runs it, such as `24940` and `"Hetzner Online GmbH"`. A customer on a hosting company's network is often a bot or someone hiding behind a server. They are read only, and `None` in a payload that leaves them out.

## 0.2.1 - 2026-11-12

- `invoices.create()` takes `success_url` and `cancel_url`, public https:// addresses the invoice's page and its charges' checkout offer the customer a way back to: a button once they have paid, and a link while it is open. `invoice.success_url` and `invoice.cancel_url` read them back, and are `None` in webhooks, which leave them out.

## 0.2.0 - 2026-11-12

- Amounts given as numbers are sent as decimal strings without an exponent: the amounts of charges, refunds, simulated payments, invoices and their items, payment links and coupons, and the payment tolerances. A float is rounded to 14 significant digits first, the precision the API reads a number with, so `0.00005` is sent as `"0.00005"` rather than as the `5e-05` the API refused, and `0.1 + 0.2` as `"0.3"`. A float that is not a number, or is infinite, raises an `InvalidArgumentError` before anything is sent. Metadata is sent as it is given.
- A `datetime` is sent in UTC, such as `created_from` and `created_to` or an `expires_at`, so one in another time zone means the same moment to the API, which read its time as UTC and moved the filter by the offset. A `datetime` without a time zone is taken as UTC, where it raised an `InvalidArgumentError`. A `date` is still sent as its day.
- `activity.list()` and `iterator()` take a `date` for `from` and `to`, and a `datetime` as its day in its own time zone; `ActivityListParams` types them as `str | date`.
- Enums for the other values the API uses: `Currency`, `MemberRole`, `AddressNetwork` (the networks `payout_addresses.update()` and `delete()` take), `CheckoutAppearance`, `WebhookDeliveryStatus`, `PlanName`, `AccountType`, `CheckoutLocale` and `ActivityAction`, every action the activity log records, `team.second_factor_requirement_changed` included. `MemberRole.OWNER` only appears in answers: invitations and role changes take the other four. Every parameter that takes one of these values, or one of the earlier enums', is typed as the enum or a `str`, and a plain string still works; the attributes of answers stay strings, since the API can add values.
- The overview's other figures are typed: `overview.period` (`from_`, since `from` is a Python keyword, and `to`), `funnel`, `attention`, `assets`, `top_payment_links`, `setup` and `daily`, as `OverviewPeriod`, `OverviewFunnel` with `OverviewFunnelAsset`, `OverviewAttention` with `OverviewChargeAmount` and `OverviewWaitingPayouts`, `OverviewAsset`, `OverviewPaymentLink`, `OverviewSetup` and `OverviewDay`. Amounts stay strings, and days are `date` objects. `get()` still reads them as the API sent them.
- What the API sends under `meta` can be read. Every `Page` has `meta`, with `mode` for the lists that say it (the coins on offer and the activity log) and `currencies`, the currencies `assets.list()` can price coins in. `payouts.summary()` answers with a `PayoutSummaryList` and `payout_addresses.list()` with a `PayoutAddressList`: lists as before, which also have `meta` and `mode`, and on the payout addresses `api_payout_address_changes`, whether an owner lets API keys change them. `overview.retrieve()` has `mode` too.
- `charge.payment_link_title` and `charge.invoice_number`, which the charges in webhooks carry beside the payment link's and the invoice's codes. They are `None` for the charges the API answers with, which give the codes alone.
- `ConflictError.was_replayed()`, true when an earlier request with the same idempotency key succeeded but its answer carried a secret, which the API shows once and does not replay: the key of `api_keys.create()`, or the signing secret of `webhook_endpoints.create()` and `rotate_secret()`. A retry after a lost answer gets this conflict, which means the write was carried out; their docstrings and the README say how to recover. The README said a repeated key always gets the first response, which is not true for these three.
- Cloudflare's 520 to 524, its answers when it gets nothing from VeliraPay, are retried like 502, 503 and 504, reads and writes alike, since a retried write carries the same idempotency key.
- The README covers the whole library, each part with an example that runs: the coins on offer and the lowest price in each currency, charges with their customer details, the network surcharge and Lightning's `payment_request`, short and late payments, invoices, payment links, coupons, payouts and payout addresses, events, webhooks and their endpoints, the account and its payment settings, the overview, the activity log, API keys, members and invitations, Pro, the enums, and testing an integration. Its charge examples no longer use the bare order number as the idempotency key, which would send a customer coming back after the charge expired to that expired charge for a day: reuse a key only to retry the same request, and give a new attempt, such as a charge replacing an expired one, a new key.

### Breaking

- `activity.list()` and `iterator()` raise an `InvalidArgumentError` for a `from` or `to` that is not a day written as YYYY-MM-DD, such as `"2026-11-01T00:00:00Z"`, before anything is sent: the API ignored it and listed the whole log. A blank one is still sent, and read as no limit.

## 0.1.0 - 2026-11-12

The first version, covering what the PHP library covers as of its 0.6.5:

- `VeliraPayClient` with `account`, `assets`, `charges`, `payment_links`, `invoices`, `coupons`, `payouts`, `payout_addresses`, `events`, `webhook_endpoints`, `overview`, `activity`, `api_keys`, `members`, `invitations` and `billing`, each answering with typed objects that keep every attribute the API sent (`get()`, `to_dict()`).
- Parameters as dicts under the API's own names, typed with a `TypedDict` per endpoint, in which a key set to `None` is sent as `null`. Enum members, a `datetime` with a time zone, a `date` and a `Decimal` are sent the way the API expects.
- `Page` for lists, with `next_page()` and `auto_paging_iterator()`, and `iterator()` on every paged list.
- An error per kind of failure, all of them a `VeliraPayError`: `AuthenticationError`, `PermissionDeniedError`, `NotFoundError`, `ConflictError`, `ValidationError`, `RateLimitError`, `ServerError`, `InvalidRequestError`, `UnexpectedResponseError`, `ApiConnectionError`, `SignatureVerificationError` and `InvalidArgumentError`.
- Retries of network errors, rate limits and outages, with an `Idempotency-Key` on every write so a retried one is carried out once.
- `Webhook.construct_event()`, which checks the signature, the second one sent for a day after a secret is rotated, and its age.
- `Asset` also has `BTC_LIGHTNING`, `ETH_BASE`, `ETH_ARBITRUM` and `POL`, which the API defines, charges and coins read the `network_surcharge` the API sends, and charges their `payment_request`.
- Requests through `urllib` from the standard library, or any transport you give the client, for Python 3.11 or later, without dependencies.
